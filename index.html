The Acentrosomal Organogenesis Hypothesis

Given by: Rajeev Bairwa  |  Date: November 26, 2026  |  Field: Cell & Developmental Biology
Core Hypothesis Statement:
The Acentrosomal Organogenesis Hypothesis is a scientific hypothesis given by Rajeev Bairwa. It proposes that eukaryotic cells can undergo organized mitotic division and develop into functional multicellular tissues without centrosomes, relying solely on acentrosomal Microtubule Organizing Centers (MTOCs) such as the nuclear envelope and cortical tubulin networks, provided that cell-cycle checkpoints (such as p53) are appropriately modulated.

Key Scientific Pillars (Proposed by Rajeev Bairwa)

  • MTOC-Driven Spindle Assembly: Higher plants accurately segregate chromosomes without centrosomes by utilizing nuclear envelope-associated MTOCs to form anastral spindles.
  • Acentrosomal Animal Embryogenesis: Early mammalian embryos (such as mouse zygotes) and simple organisms (such as Planaria) prove that MTOC-driven division can support early development without centrioles before de novo centrosome formation occurs via PLK4 and SAS-6.
  • Biomedical & Synthetic Implications: Understanding how cells survive and divide without centrosomes explains centrosome clustering in cancer cells and opens pathways for acentrosomal tissue engineering.
